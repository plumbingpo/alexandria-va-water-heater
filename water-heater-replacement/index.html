Signs You Need Water Heater Replacement in Alexandria

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Each tank needs a properly rated relief valve whose discharge pipe ends somewhere safe, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps to meet local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, you will hear it from us directly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
