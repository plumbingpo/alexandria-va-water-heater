Water heater installation covers more ground than a simple swap. Plenty of Alexandria, VA projects start with a finished basement or a new addition that needs its own heater. Other jobs involve moving a unit out of a cramped closet, stepping up to a bigger tank for a growing family, or switching fuels entirely. Each of those jobs involves new gas piping, new vent routes, new circuits, or new water lines, and each one has to be planned before the first pipe is cut. We take on installations of every size, from a small point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Before any pipe is cut, we match the heater to your household. Tanks are compared by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and tankless units are sized by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your water needs in winter. Next, we choose the spot together. Gas models require a vent route outdoors and adequate combustion air. Heat pump models require breathing room, a space that stays above roughly 40 degrees, and a place to send condensate. Electric tankless models often require a dedicated high-amperage circuit. Anywhere a leak could ruin finished space, a drain pan goes underneath. You get clear choices and a firm price before work begins.

Installation day runs on a checklist: gas lines sized, run, and leak-tested with a sediment trap at the heater, vents built to the manufacturer's size and pitch, circuits and breakers matched to the heater's load, and water lines connected with shutoffs and dielectric protection between copper and steel. A full-size relief valve discharge line runs to a safe point, and any required expansion tank, drain pan, seismic straps, or permit is part of the job. Before we leave, we fill and purge the tank, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, set the temperature near 120 degrees, and walk you through everything. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household anymore
  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water
  • Venting, relief valve piping, or gas connections that do not look right
  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Installing a new water heater means doing several trades' work at once. The gas piping must be sized for the heater plus every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested until it is leak-free. Venting has to follow the manufacturer's rules for diameter, length, pitch, and clearance to combustibles, or carbon monoxide can spill back into the house. Electric and heat pump units need the right circuit, breaker, and wire gauge, and a whole-house electric tankless unit can demand more current than many panels can spare. Then come the water-side details: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that vary by location.

With a professional install, every piece is done right once. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your demand, calculate the gas line, plan the vent route, confirm electrical capacity, and install the code items your location requires. Leaks, draft, and carbon monoxide are tested, and the heater is left at a safe temperature with the paperwork handled. That is the difference between a heater that simply works for years and one that fails inspection or backdrafts on the first cold morning. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
